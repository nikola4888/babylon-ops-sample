Outside sample memo: Lombard

Based only on public materials, checked 9 October 2026. Not affiliated with Lombard. Not a client engagement.

Written by Nikola

A shorter contrast to the Babylon plan, built around one question: who is this community for now? Lombard's LBTC began as a liquid staking token earning from Babylon. This year its yield source moved to an options strategy.

What the public product is now

The homepage calls Lombard onchain Bitcoin infrastructure and lists six products: LBTC, BTC.b, Bitcoin Smart Accounts, Bitcoin Earn, a Bitcoin Onchain Credit Strategy, and the Lombard SDK. It sorts visitors into Bitcoin holders, institutional allocators and integration partners.[1]

The banner is the change. From the week of 17 August, LBTC's yield comes from a covered-call options strategy managed by Bitwise, targeting 2.5% net APY in Bitcoin terms, “variable and not guaranteed”. Up to 60% of LBTC's backing goes into that strategy, held with qualified custodians; the rest stays with Lombard's Security Consortium. Lombard says holders need do nothing: the token contract, mint and redeem logic and bridge are unchanged.[2]

The announcement gives the reason in one sentence: Bitcoin staking as a market “has failed to develop”, and its yield is “unsustainable and likely to decline further”.[2] It also states that the protocol is not intended for US or UK persons.[2]

The shift, and where the record still shows it

The docs tell the story in a short table. Before July 2025, holders claimed BABY, Babylon's staking reward token, manually in the Lombard app. From 22 July 2025, yield auto-compounded into LBTC's exchange rate. In 2026, the yield source moved from Babylon staking to the Bitwise strategy. BABY left unclaimed before July 2025 was redistributed to holders.[3]

The shift still shows elsewhere. Babylon's own BTC Staking Tools page still lists Lombard's LBTC among liquid staking tokens.[4] Lombard's blog index still carries older post text that links to discord.gg/lombardfinance, which returned “Unknown Invite” when I checked; the docs header uses a different invite.[5][6][7][8] And the chain list is shrinking: from 16 September 2026, native minting and redemption ended on TAC, Sonic, Katana, Berachain and Starknet, and holders on the first four must bridge to Ethereum by 10 December 2026.[9]

Who the community is for now

LBTC holders in DeFi. Still the core. Their questions have changed from “where are my points?” to “what is the strategy doing?”, and the answer is the daily transparency page the announcement describes.[2]

Holders who never open a DeFi app. The announcement names Bitcoin Earn products on Ledger, Binance and Bybit. Their support door is the platform they use, not Lombard's Discord.[2]

Institutions. Bitcoin Smart Accounts and the credit strategy, through the contact route on the homepage.[1]

BARD holders. A shrinking set of things to do. BARD staking closed on 17 September 2026 and rewards stopped on 1 October; unstaking happens through Mellow's interface, not the Lombard app, with a 21-day wait. The Season 3 claim at claim.lombard.finance closes on 29 October 2026.[10]

Who the old framing no longer fits. Anyone expecting BABY rewards, Babylon points or “liquid staked BTC” from LBTC. Anyone on a deprecated chain who hasn't read the update. US and UK persons.[3][9][2] They will keep arriving through old threads and old listings. They deserve a short, kind, final answer with a link.

What I would stop saying

  • “LBTC is liquid-staked BTC on Babylon.” True as history; the docs say the yield source moved in 2026.[3]
  • “2.5%” without “target, variable, not guaranteed”. Lombard never says it without them.[2]
  • “Stake your BARD.” Staking closed; the Stake function is gone from the app.[10]
  • “Unstake in the Lombard app.” It happens through Mellow now, which also means every “unstake here” link in a DM deserves suspicion.[10]
  • “Mint on Berachain” or any of the other four chains. Point to the chain update and the 10 December deadline instead.[9]
  • Any Discord link copied from an old post.[6]
