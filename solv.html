Outside sample memo: Solv

Based only on public materials, checked 9 October 2026. Not affiliated with Solv. Not a client engagement.

Written by Nikola

A shorter contrast to the Babylon plan. I expected a narrow Bitcoin-wrapper site. The product surface is wide; what is thin is the routing between its parts, and between the campaigns that ended and the ones still running.

What is actually public today

The homepage calls Solv an on-chain Bitcoin reserve and ranks it against other Bitcoin treasuries, citing data dated 11 May 2026. It speaks to traders using SolvBTC as collateral, holders using exchange earn programs, DeFi users of xSolvBTC, and people who want structured BTC yield. It also points to weekly reserve proofs, monthly custodian attestations, MiCA alignment and Shariah principles.[1]

The docs carry SolvBTC, xSolvBTC, BTC+, Solv Vaults and LSTs, Bitcoin Reserve Offerings, a Staking Abstraction Layer, the SOLV token, campaigns and two points seasons.[2] SolvBTC has two separate sections in the index, one under its own name and one at a URL ending “solvbtc-1”, and they describe it differently.[2][3]

The channel list is short and clear: the docs introduction names the website, X, Discord, Telegram and Medium.[4] What the list doesn't say is which of them carries support.[5][6][7]

What is thin is the routing between all of that:

  • Four answers to “how fast can I get out?”: SolvBTC redemptions are processed the Monday after you ask; xSolvBTC has instant redemption to SolvBTC; the MegaDrop guide says unstaking takes 2 to 7 days; the homepage says “100% quick and easy redemption”.[3][8][9][1]
  • The User Guide section has two pages: a Binance MegaDrop quest and a BABY claim whose last linking date was 9 June 2025.[2][9][10]
  • The Venus borrowing campaign ended on 31 May 2025. Its page still says the claim window is “TBD”.[11]
  • BTC+ is described as having “no off-chain venues, no custodial counterparties”. The homepage points to custodian attestations and delta-neutrality proofs. Both can be true of different products; a newcomer has no page that says which is which.[12][1]
  • Babylon's staking tools table lists “Solv: SolvBTC.BBN”. Solv's LST Suite page doesn't list that name, and its BABY guide talks about xSolvBTC.[13][14][10]

What I would set up in the first 30 days

  1. One public “which Solv product do I hold?” page, built only from Solv's own links: SolvBTC, xSolvBTC, BTC+, each LST, with its redemption route and timing quoted from its own docs page.
  2. A pinned canonical-links post in Telegram, where the people actually are: one X account, one Discord invite, one docs site. Telegram is the largest listed room, so it is the one impersonators copy first.[6]
  3. A standing line on every campaign mention: whether it is live, ended, or waiting on a claim. Old campaigns with open questions are where fake claim links do best.[11][10]
  4. A docs-fix list, sent through whatever channel the team names: the two SolvBTC sections, the Venus “TBD”, the 2025 user guides, the SolvBTC.BBN name. Reported, not posted.
  5. A weekly note of questions repeated in Telegram and Discord, each paired with the doc link it should have pointed to.

What I would not build yet

  • A Discord programme. I would find out where support actually happens before building anywhere.[7][6]
  • Answers about reserve rankings, MiCA or Shariah status beyond quoting the homepage. Those are claims for Solv's legal and research teams, not a moderator.[1]
  • Points or vSOLV campaign copy. The docs list several campaigns, and I didn't verify which are current.[2]
  • Any post that repeats a yield number. The homepage counters loaded as zero for me, and I would link to the app rather than quote it.
  • Separate rooms per product. Until the product page has run for a month, splitting rooms is guessing.
