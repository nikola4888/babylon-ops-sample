Campaign teardown: Babylon’s vault-first fortnight

Based only on public materials, checked 9 October 2026. Not affiliated with Babylon. Not a client engagement.

Written by Nikola

Four Babylon posts and one partner repost in two weeks: “Bitcoin Borrowing, Repriced” on 25 September with Aegis's testnet the same day, a new one-line positioning on 6 October, HashKey Cloud on 7 October, and “TBV is the CPU, not the phone” on 8 October.[1][2][3][4][5] Babylon didn't call this a campaign. I'm reading it as one sequence because every post is about vaults, and none is about staking.[6]

What the sequence was for

“Repriced” makes the economic case. It sets centralized lenders' rate cards and wrapped Bitcoin's trust costs against borrowing with native BTC through Aave v4, and walks through the five steps: lock, activate, borrow, repay, unlock. It says plainly that the flow is available on the public testnet with test assets.[1]

The Aegis repost shows a second product on the same rails. Fixed-rate loans against BTC, on Bitcoin signet and Ethereum Sepolia.[2]

The 6 October post is a new tagline. “Infrastructure for Native Bitcoin Credit Market”, with no other text.[3]

HashKey adds an institutional distributor. HashKey Cloud will integrate vaults for its institutional clients. The post ends: “Further details on the integration timing and partnership will be shared in the coming months.”[4][7]

The CPU post tells people what Babylon is not. Not the app the end user sees; the part inside it.[5]

Read together: Babylon is now a credit-infrastructure company, and the people who will use it may never see its name. That is a coherent message for partners. It is a confusing one for the community that already has BTC staked through Babylon.

None of the five speaks to a current staker. The one most likely to generate questions is HashKey, because “will integrate” reads as “is live” in a hurry, and the vault product is still on testnet.[4][8] The other date worth knowing: a year earlier, Babylon's vault-first roadmap aimed at a mainnet launch “early next year”.[9] Long-time holders remember that line, and “in the coming months” lands on top of it.

What I would test next

No targets. Each test compares the questions people ask in public before and after one change, read by hand.

  1. A status line on every vault post

    “Public testnet: signet and Sepolia, test assets only.” Under partner posts too. Watch whether “is this live?” replies slow down.[8]

  2. One staking post a fortnight

    Even with nothing new: where to check a stake, which page has unbonding times. Watch whether “is staking ending?” stops appearing in replies to vault posts.

  3. A dated roadmap note

    One public page that says what the 2025 roadmap expected and where things stand now. Watch whether mainnet-date questions move from accusation to inquiry.[9]

What the posts do not prove

  • That anyone acted on them. When I checked, X showed between about 5,900 and 13,400 impressions for each of Babylon's own posts. Impressions are views, not vaults opened or loans taken.[10][3][7][5]
  • That HashKey's clients will use vaults, or when. The post says the timing is still to come.[4]
  • That the rate comparison will hold. “Repriced” quotes other lenders' rate cards as of its date; rates move.[1]
  • That stakers were ignored on purpose. A fortnight of vault posts may simply be the calendar.
  • The blog's own dates. On the index page, several older posts show “October 1, 2026”, so I dated each post from its own page.[11]
  • Intent. I have described what the posts do, not what Babylon meant them to do.
